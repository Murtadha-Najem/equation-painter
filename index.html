How it paints

A soft mask

e−ez

Close to 1 where z is negative, close to 0 where it is positive. Put an ellipse inside z and you get a stencil; the factor k in front sets how sharp its edge is, from a soft glow at 2 to a crisp line at 32. Every term is one of these.

Three colours from one line

Cj(v) = (a + bv + cv²)/d
v = 0v = 1v = 2

A quadratic in v passes through the red, green and blue value of a term, so one expression gives all three channels.

Search, then round

θ = 1.57, p = 0.21, k = 16

Each new term tries random spots, favouring the ones furthest from your picture, then nudges the best. The time you pick sets how hard each term searches: a longer budget reads the picture in finer detail and places every term more carefully. Numbers are rounded to two decimals as it goes, so the formula printed is exactly the one that drew.

What it does and does not do. The formula stores no pixels: each term is nine numbers, so even a 1,000 term picture carries about nine thousand constants against hundreds of thousands of pixels. The search does read your image to measure the difference, as any automatic fit must. Yeganeh designs each term by hand and each one means something, an eye or a fin; here each term is a patch the search chose.
